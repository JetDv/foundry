# Gate heuristics

How to tell a boundary from a habit, and one job from two. Applies only while drafting the stage table.

## A gate is real when

- The person **stops and looks** before continuing — not "I glance at it", but "I don't send it until I've checked X".
- **Correction there is cheaper than correction downstream.** An outline is cheap to fix; a finished draft built on a wrong outline is not. This is the whole argument for early gates.
- **They can say what they are checking for.** If "review it" is the best they can do, push: review it for what? The answer is the human check line. If there is no answer, there is no gate.

## A gate is fake when

- It exists because the workflow diagram looked lopsided.
- It sits between two steps the person does in one sitting without pausing.
- The check is "make sure it's good."

Fake gates are worse than no gates: they train the person to click through, and once clicking through is the habit, the real gates get clicked through too.

## One job or two

A stage does one job if you can state it in five words with no "and". Watch for the hidden and:

- "research and outline" — two jobs. Research is gathering; outlining is a structural decision. The outline is the cheapest thing in the pipeline to correct, which is exactly why it deserves its own gate.
- "draft and format" — two jobs, and the second one belongs to a reference file more than a stage.
- "review and publish" — two jobs, and the first is a human check, not a stage.

## The U-curve

`../../../_shared/forms.md`, Pipeline, describes the U-curve of human editing. The consequence for stage design: the first and last outputs should be the easiest files in the workspace to edit — short, plainly formatted, no nested structure a person has to preserve by hand. A first-stage output that is painful to edit will not get edited, and everything downstream inherits the error.

## Sequencing

Numbering encodes order and nothing else. If two stages could run in either order, they are probably one stage, or the second one's inputs are underspecified. Find out which before numbering them.
